## 1. US COPPA Rule (16 CFR Part 312) and the 2025 amendments

### Takeaway
A username+password account with no email, phone, name, DOB or cross-site identifier is, on the text of the Rule, probably not "personal information" unless the username functions like online contact information, so verifiable parental consent (VPC) is probably not triggered. The residual COPPA risks are free-text fields where children volunteer PII, IP/identifiers in logs, and the status of "mixed audience / directed to children" for an app aimed at ~8-year-olds (it will almost certainly be "directed to children"). The 2025 amendments add written security, retention-policy and separate-disclosure-consent duties, with a compliance deadline that has now passed (22 Apr 2026).

### Cited Findings
- Dates: final amendments published in the Federal Register 22 Apr 2025; effective 23 Jun 2025; compliance deadline 22 Apr 2026 for most provisions (safe-harbor programs earlier). Deadline already passed as of today. — [Lowenstein Sandler summary](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/ftc-finalizes-updates-to-coppa-rule-what-you-need-to-know); [Ashurst/Perkins Coie summary](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/ftc-finally-publishes-amended-coppa-rule-compliance-deadlines-set/). Federal Register doc no. 2025-05904 (per search result; page itself not retrievable).
- Changes (secondary summary; CFR cites not given by the source): (a) "personal information" now explicitly includes biometric identifiers; (b) written information security program with a designated coordinator; (c) separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties for targeted advertising or purposes not integral to the service; (d) retention only as long as reasonably necessary, with a written retention policy and no indefinite retention; (e) safe-harbor transparency. — [Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/ftc-finalizes-updates-to-coppa-rule-what-you-need-to-know)
- eCFR 312.5(a)(2) text confirms "separate verifiable parental consent" for disclosure. — [eCFR 312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=312&section=312.5)
- Definition of "personal information" (16 CFR 312.2) lists: (1) first and last name; (2) physical address; (3) online contact information; (4) "A screen or user name where it functions in the same manner as online contact information"; (5) telephone number; (6) government identifier; (7) "A persistent identifier that can be used to recognize a user over time and across different websites or online services"; (8) photo/video/audio with a child's image or voice; (9) geolocation to street/city level; (10) biometric identifier; (11) information collected from the child and combined with an identifier above. — [eCFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=312&section=312.2)
- "Online contact information" = email or "any other substantially similar identifier that permits direct contact with a person online" (IM id, VoIP id, video-chat id; mobile number only for parental-consent texts). — [eCFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=312&section=312.2)
- "Support for the internal operations" includes activities necessary to maintain/analyze functioning, perform network communications, "authenticate users", protect security/integrity, ensure legal compliance; the data may not be used to contact a specific individual, amass a profile, or for other purposes. — [eCFR 312.2](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=312&section=312.2). Per 312.5(c)(7) the internal-operations exception from prior consent applies where information is used solely for that. — [eCFR 312.5](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=312&section=312.5)
- Mixed-audience: "A mixed audience website or online service shall not be deemed directed to children with regard to any visitor not identified as under 13" (312.2). FTC FAQ D.4: mixed audience is a subset of "directed to children"; D.3: not directed to children just because some children visit; D.6: mixed-audience operators may age-screen but may not block children altogether. — [eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/api/renderer/v1/content/enhanced/current/title-16?part=312&section=312.2); [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- FAQ A.3 / C.5: usernames count only where they function as online contact info; persistent identifiers (cookie id, IP address) are personal information unless used solely for internal operations. — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- Actual knowledge: FAQ H.1 and H.6 (a child announcing their age can create actual knowledge where posts are monitored); E.1 (prominently disclosing a contact method reduces the chance of knowledge through other employees). — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions)
- School authorization (FAQ N.1-N.5): schools may act as the parent's agent only for use "for the use and benefit of the school, and for no other commercial purpose"; operator must give the school the notice it would give a parent, and allow review, deletion, and stopping further collection; if the operator uses data for its own commercial purposes it needs parental consent; schools/districts, not individual teachers, should decide (N.3); schools cannot consent if they cannot review/delete data or if data is used for behavioural advertising/profiling (N.5). — [FTC COPPA FAQ](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). Note: this is FTC guidance, not Rule text; 312.5 as retrieved contains no school language. FAQ page as retrieved did not reflect the 2025 amendments (page notes only a Jan 2025 penalty edit); check for an updated FAQ. [CONFIRM]
- Enforcement 2024-26 relevant to edtech: FTC proposed order against Illuminate Education 4 Dec 2025 (finalized 5 Jun 2026 per aggregator): Section 5 data-security case, requires comprehensive security programme and assessments; NY/CA/CT AG settlement $5.1m 6 Nov 2025. Brought as unfairness/deception, not COPPA. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/education/1717146/ftcs-proposed-consent-order-with-illuminate-education-a-tale-of-two-enforcement-approaches); [compliancehub (low authority)](https://compliancehub.wiki/ftc-illuminate-education-infosec-order-edtech-2026/). FTC also announced COPPA actions against Disney and Apitor (Sept 2025; headlines only, terms not verified). FTC 2022 edtech policy statement (committing to enforce COPPA limits on edtech) mentioned by [Sourcepoint](https://sourcepoint.com/blog/ftc-to-enforce-coppa-in-edtech) (secondary). Policy statement 25 Feb 2026 limiting enforcement for age-verification data collected without prior consent — [Davis Polk via search summary, not directly read]. [CONFIRM]

### Inferences
- If the app collects only a chosen username, a password hash, a class label and dates, no 312.2 category is met, provided the username is not an email/handle that "permits direct contact". Mitigation: block "@", digits-that-look-like-phones, and suggest pseudonym generators/random names. A child entering a real name as username is a "first and last name" risk (item 1) if it reads as such, and the operator cannot reliably detect it; the FAQ H.6 knowledge risk is small without monitoring.
- IP address in a 15-minute rate-limit window and in 30-day logs is a persistent identifier; it is plausibly covered by "internal operations" (security, network communications), provided it is not used for anything else. Document that purpose.
- The feedback form is the main COPPA exposure: free text from under-13s can include names/contact details even if scrubbed. Scrubbing and no account linkage lower risk; the 312.5(c)(3) one-time-response exception does not fit (no reply). Consider an "ask a grown-up / don't type your name" prompt and teacher-mediated submission, or disable for under-13 cohorts.
- App will likely be "directed to children" (age ~8 learners, per product description). No age screen is useful then; the strategy is data minimisation so no VPC is required.
- With no third-party disclosure (verify that Cloud Run/logging vendors are processors only), the new separate-disclosure-consent rule is not triggered. The written retention policy and written security programme requirements apply to "operators" collecting personal information; if nothing qualifies as PI, arguably not applicable, but adopt a short written version anyway (cheap, and used for DPDP).
- COPPA applies to foreign operators directing services at US children (background, not re-verified); US exposure is low in a 1,000-user invite-only beta unless US schools are invited.

### Gaps
- Could not read Federal Register text (blocked) for exact CFR cites for retention (312.10), security (312.8), and the amended 312.5(a)(2); I did not verify cites beyond 312.2/312.5.
- No verified FTC 2024-26 action specifically about username-only accounts.
- State laws (California AADC, SOPIPA, NY, etc.; student-privacy laws in OK, ID, AZ are mentioned only in FAQ N.1) not researched; relevant if US schools adopt.

## 2. EU / UK

### Takeaway
Pseudonymous data is still personal data in the GDPR sense (background), but a design that keeps no direct identifiers, no profiling and short retention puts the app in a strong position. Article 8 only bites if consent is the legal basis for an information society service offered directly to a child; legitimate interest/contract (via school) may avoid it. UK Children's Code reaches non-UK services likely to be accessed by UK children but practical exposure is very low for an invite-only, India-targeted beta.

### Cited Findings
- GDPR Art. 8(1): where consent is the basis and the service is offered directly to a child, processing is lawful if the child is at least 16; below that, consent by the holder of parental responsibility; Member States may lower the age but not below 13. Art. 8(2): controller must make reasonable efforts to verify parental consent, taking technology into account. Art. 8(3): contract law unaffected. — [GDPR Art. 8 (gdpr-info.eu, summary)](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-8-gdpr/)
- UK Children's Code: applies to services "likely to be accessed" by under-18s in the UK ("more probable than not" reading per law-firm commentary), not only child-directed services; commentary says it reaches providers outside the UK and lists educational websites in scope. — [Mondaq summary](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/privacy-protection/1109232/online-safety-the-icos-childrens-code); [Reed Smith on draft scope guidance](https://viewpoints.reedsmith.com/post/102ibpj/new-draft-uk-ico-guidance-on-scope-of-the-childrens-code). ICO pages were not retrievable; secondary sources date from 2020-2021. [CONFIRM current ICO scope guidance]
- Member-State ages (e.g., 13 UK/Denmark/Belgium, 14 Spain/Italy, 15 France, 16 Germany/Netherlands/Ireland is 16) (background, not re-verified; check current).
- Art. 25 data protection by design/default; Art. 35 DPIA triggers (systematic monitoring, large-scale, vulnerable data subjects/children as vulnerable per EDPB/WP29 guidance); Art. 3(2) extraterritorial scope for offering services to people in the EU; Art. 27 EU representative (exempt for occasional processing unlikely to risk rights); Recital 26 and Art. 4(5) pseudonymisation: pseudonymous data remains personal data when re-identification is reasonably possible; Art. 4(1). All (background, not re-verified in this session). [CONFIRM]
- ICO Children's Code 15 standards (best interests, DPIA, age appropriate application, transparency, detrimental use, policies and community standards, default settings, data minimisation, data sharing, geolocation, parental controls, profiling, nudge techniques, connected toys, online tools) (background, not re-verified).

### Inferences
- Art. 3(2) turns on "offering" to people in the EU/UK; an invite-only beta distributed through Indian schools almost certainly does not offer to EU data subjects. If any EU/UK school or family is invited, Art. 3(2) and UK GDPR likely apply and the Code's standards (minimisation, high-privacy defaults, no profiling, no nudging, DPIA-lite, child-friendly notice) become the benchmark. The design already meets most.
- A username+password-hash+class label is personal data (identifiable/singling out), so lawful basis is needed. Avoid consent as the basis where a school invites; use legitimate interests with a documented balancing test, or public task/contract through the school [CONFIRM].
- Feedback form without account link and with scrubbing: arguably anonymous in practice, but free text may include personal data; treat as personal data until scrubbed.
- DPIA-lite: not mandatory at 1,000 users, but children plus a possible future AI tutor suggest doing one before enabling the tutor.

### Gaps
- No primary-source verification of EDPB guidance or ICO current text (403). Member-state age list unverified.

## 3. India: DPDP Act 2023 and DPDP Rules 2025

### Takeaway
India is the operator's home law and the strictest on children: any person under 18 is a "child" and s.9 bans tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising regardless of consent. Rules were notified 13/14 Nov 2025; the children and notice/security/breach obligations only commence 18 months later (about 13-14 May 2027), unless MeitY's floated acceleration is notified. Design the beta to comply now; it will be live under the law before the product matures.

### Cited Findings
- Notification: DPDP Rules 2025 notified in Gazette 13 Nov 2025 (G.S.R. 846(E) per [one wiki](https://dpdp.myndsolution.com/wiki/rules/schedule-4-classes-of-data-fiduciaries-in-respect-of-whom-provisions-of/); other sources say 14 Nov, e.g. [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/)). Date conflict noted; Gazette not retrieved. [CONFIRM]
- Phasing: Rules 1, 2, 17-21 in force on publication; Rule 4 (consent managers) one year after (13 Nov 2026, i.e. now); Rules 3, 5-16, 22, 23 eighteen months after publication (approx. 13-14 May 2027). — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/); [Legal500/others via search](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/privacy/dpdp-act-and-rules-2025-the-2026-compliance-milestones-businesses-cant-afford-to-miss)
- Proposed acceleration: MeitY floated at a 22 Jan 2026 stakeholder meeting cutting 18 months to 12 and activating some rules sooner; reports differ on scope; as of latest sources (Sept 2026) no Gazette amendment found. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/meity-seeks-industry-views-on-fast-tracking-dpdp-act-rollout-proposes-12-month-compliance-timeline-88332.htm); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-plans-to-cut-short-dpdp-compliance-timeline-and-notify-cross-border-restrictions-for-sdfs/). Treat as unresolved. [CONFIRM on meity.gov.in / egazette]
- Act s.9 (secondary summary; wording not verified against the Gazette): 9(1) verifiable consent of parent/lawful guardian before processing a child's data; 9(2) no processing likely to cause detrimental effect on a child's well-being; 9(3) no tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at them; 9(4) rules may exempt classes of fiduciary/purposes from 9(1) and 9(3); 9(5) government may notify a higher age for verifiably safe processors. — [dpdpa.com s.9](https://dpdpa.com/dpdpa2023/chapter-2/section9.html) (unofficial). One commentary reports that 9(2) is not exempted.
- Child = under 18 (background; s.2(f), not re-verified).
- Rule 10 (per SCC Online): verifiable parental consent via reliable identification, parent age/identity verification, Digital Locker/authorised token services. Rule 11: persons with disabilities. Rule 12 with Fourth Schedule: limited exemptions. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/). Rule numbering for exemptions conflicts across sources. [CONFIRM]
- Fourth Schedule: educational institutions (and daycares/crèches) exempt from consent and from the tracking/behavioural monitoring ban only for tracking/monitoring "for educational activities" and "ensuring the safety of children enrolled"; narrow, not a licence for analytics or marketing. Healthcare and others also listed. — [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/digital/dpdp-rules-carve-out-key-exemptions-for-healthcare-providers-schools-and-childcare-services-processing-childrens-data-84208.htm); [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/schools-coaching-centres-platforms-processing-childrens-data-are-you-compliant/) (secondary; verbatim not verified). The exemption attaches to the educational institution as fiduciary; whether a third-party app operator can rely on it (vs. acting as the school's processor) is not settled in sources found.
- Duties (per SCC Online): Rule 3 notice in clear plain language, standalone, with contact details and rights; Rule 6 reasonable security safeguards (encryption/obfuscation, access control, logging, monitoring, backups); Rule 7 breach notice to affected principals without delay and to the Board (reported as within hours, details staged; check Rule text); Rule 8 erasure when purpose is served, 48-hour pre-erasure notice to principal, retain logs and related data at least one year. — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/). Note the one-year log retention in Rule 8 (as summarised) conflicts with a 30-day log policy; the retention applies to logs of personal data processing relevant to Rule 6 [CONFIRM exact wording, and whether it applies to a small fiduciary].
- Applicability of "personal data": defined as any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data (background, s.2(t); not re-verified). A chosen username plus class label identifies a person in context to the school/admin; treat as personal data.
- CERT-In Directions of 28 Apr 2022 (s.70B(6) IT Act): 6-hour incident reporting; maintain ICT logs for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction; FAQs 18 May 2022. Applicability described by commentators as "service providers, intermediaries, data centres, body corporates, government organisations"; exact reach unclear. — [Nishith Desai](https://nishithdesai.com/research-and-articles/hotline/technology-law-analysis/cert-in-releases-faqs-explaining-the-direction-on-cybersecurity-6139); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/it-and-internet/1270362/6-hours-to-report-cyber-incidents-to-cert-in-and-more-compliances); [Cyril Amarchand](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2022/05/the-cert-in-cyber-security-directions-more-questions-than-answers/). A source says FAQs allow log copies outside India if producible; conflicting. [CONFIRM]

### Inferences
- The operator is a Data Fiduciary for its own account data. A school may be a fiduciary for its students; the operator may be a processor under contract (DPDP s.8(2) background) or independent fiduciary. Decide and document.
- Because s.9(1) has no exemption for ordinary app operators (the Fourth Schedule covers schools), a no-PII design does not remove the need for verifiable parental consent from 13 May 2027 unless the data is not "personal data" or the school-as-fiduciary model is used. Cheapest route: school is fiduciary and consents/administers (school obtains parental consent per Rule 10), operator acts as processor under a data-processing agreement; absence of tracking/profiling keeps s.9(3) clean.
- 30-day logs vs CERT-In 180 days: if CERT-In directions apply to the operator (cloud-hosted application provider/body corporate is plausible), 30 days is non-compliant on its face. Counsel to decide; low-cost fix is keeping Cloud Run request logs (with IP) 180 days in an Indian region, restricted access, which contradicts minimisation. This is a genuine tension to escalate. Aggregate analytics must not become "behavioural monitoring" of children (s.9(3)); per-person event logs are absent, which helps.
- Cross-border: DPDP s.16 allows transfer except to restricted countries notified by government (background); no restrictions found notified; hosting in India is simplest.

## 4. Practical implications

1. Username+password: low-risk if the username is non-contact-like. If a child uses a real name, it becomes personal data under every regime, and name + class label identifies them; mitigate by suggestions/generated names, a notice telling children not to use real names, allowing admin-forced rename. Password: store hashed (argon2/bcrypt); no recovery email means admin-mediated reset, which needs an identity check by the teacher.
2. Feedback form: highest content risk (children may disclose names, health, family issues, safeguarding issues). Scrubbing is partial; no account link and 180-day deletion help. Add prompt "don't include your name or contact details," a visible notice, and a safeguarding path (if disclosures of harm appear, who reads it and what to do) (background best practice).
3. Aggregate analytics and 30-day logs: fine under COPPA internal-operations language if used only for security/operation; under GDPR needs lawful basis and disclosure; under DPDP s.9(3) avoid per-child behaviour monitoring. Reconcile 30 days vs CERT-In/DPDP Rule 8 one-year log language [CONFIRM].
4. Schools/teachers: US FAQ says school (not teacher) should decide, and the operator should give the school the direct notice and allow review/delete; DPDP Fourth Schedule exemption sits with the institution; India parental consent per Rule 10 will be needed by May 2027 unless exempt. Provide a one-page school/teacher notice and a simple DPA.
5. Retention: 180-day feedback, 2-month expiry plus 90-day purge. Fits COPPA "no longer than reasonably necessary" and GDPR storage limitation if the written schedule justifies each period; DPDP Rule 8 (as summarised) requires a 48-hour pre-erasure notice, which is hard with no contact data (could be met by an in-app/teacher notice). Document.
6. Cross-border hosting: GDPR Chapter V applies when EU data goes to a non-EEA host (background); with Indian operator + any host, transfer tools needed only if EU learners exist. DPDP s.16 black-list model (background). CERT-In wants logs in India [CONFIRM]. Choose an Indian Cloud Run region (asia-south1 Mumbai or asia-south2 Delhi) for simplicity.

## 5. Minimal compliance artefacts for a 1,000-user beta
- Child-friendly privacy notice (reading age ~8; short, plain, what is/isn't collected, who to ask) and a parent/teacher notice (fuller); DPDP Rule 3 requires notice in clear, plain language.
- Data inventory/map (fields, purpose, location, retention, who sees).
- Retention schedule (accounts, feedback 180 d, logs, rate-limit 15 min, backups).
- DPIA-lite (1-2 pages; mandatory before AI tutor is enabled).
- Security measures list (hashing, TLS, access control, secrets, least privilege, backups) mapped to COPPA written programme and DPDP Rule 6.
- Contact point (named person or grievance email on site) – DPDP grievance redressal and COPPA FAQ E.1.
- Breach/incident plan (who, 6-hour CERT-In clock if applicable, DPDP Board/principal notice, school notice, GDPR 72-hour if EU).
- School/teacher data-processing terms; account deletion/export process by admin.
- Subprocessor list (Google Cloud) and written statement that no data is shared for advertising.

## 6. Prioritised checklist (lowest cost first)
1. Decide controller/processor model with schools; get a short school agreement and have the school confirm parental notification/consent (covers FAQ N.1-N.5 and DPDP Rule 10 path).
2. Username rules: reject email-like/phone-like strings, provide generated pseudonyms, notice "do not use your real name."
3. Feedback form: warning text, keep scrub, keep no linkage, consider off for under-13 or teacher-moderated; define who reads it.
4. Publish child notice + parent/teacher notice + contact point.
5. Written retention schedule and security statement (one page each).
6. Resolve log retention: get counsel opinion on CERT-In applicability to the operator; align log policy and DPDP Rule 8 language.
7. Keep AI tutor off until a DPIA, a vendor review (no training on child data), and the s.9(2)/(3) analysis are done.
8. Keep hosting in an Indian region; restrict log access; no third-party analytics/fonts/trackers (verify front end loads none).
9. Re-check by 13 May 2027 (or sooner if MeitY amends Rule 1) for DPDP commencement; monitor FTC FAQ updates for 2025 amendments.
10. Don't invite EU/UK users without a short GDPR/Children's Code check.

## Other risks noticed
- Password reset without contact data: weak social-engineering path; require teacher/admin verification.
- Browser-only progress means no server profile, which supports minimisation, but shared school computers can leak progress between pupils.
- Invite links distributed by teachers: ensure single-use/expiring to prevent adults or strangers joining.
- The "no PII" claim should be stated carefully in public notices (say "we do not ask for", not "we hold none"), because usernames and free text can contain PII.
